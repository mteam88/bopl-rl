# Failure-mode atlas of the E0 anneal endpoint (2026-10-19)

Written by the atlas research subagent (Karpathy recipe step 1: look at the data). No GPU used.
Subject: E0 l2-s301 anneal, `research/e0/l2-s301/anneal/checkpoints/policy-1333395456.bin`.
T = tp-spec, D = T/native/artifacts/rl/sweeps/atlas-2026-10 (replays, visuals, sample summaries).
Tool: `eval/failure_atlas.py` (report / replay / render). The full generated tables are in
[atlas/anneal-report.md](atlas/anneal-report.md); the contact sheets are in [atlas/](atlas/).

## Data and method

- **Discovery set:** 5904 games of `all_combos.py --games-out` (T/.../e0/l2-s301/evals/anneal-*.games.jsonl): 1076 against each
  panel net (final5-18666m, anneal20-26666m, team5b-6250m, g111-20b-26027m) and 400 against each exploiter (x2-drill,
  x2-missile, x2-blinkplat, l2-me32-trap). Both kits are random any-3 draws, there are 124 maps, and each seed is played twice
  with sides swapped.
- **Confirmation set:** 12912 games of the same run's raw-8000m, ema-8000m and raw-2666m endpoints, played on other seed bases.
  Every weak pocket found in the discovery set is re-tested here, one-sided, with Holm over the pockets.
- **Statistics:** all intervals are 95%, clustered on seed pairs. A pocket's residual is its score minus A's score against the
  same opponent, so pockets are opponent-adjusted. Pockets are pre-game facts: map, side, and each ability in A's or the
  opponent's kit. q-values are BH over all 212 pockets, with Holm beside them. Phase and death cause are outcomes, so they are
  reported descriptively.
- **Phases:** row `ticks` exclude the 240-tick warmup, so sudden death starts at row tick 6960 (116 s).
- **Replays:** `failure_atlas.py replay` reproduced all 133 games tried exactly (same ticks and outcome as the row):
  - 13 hand-picked games from the worst pockets, plus wins;
  - a random 30 per panel net (D/sample, 120 games), for behaviour statistics.
- **Rendering:** the GPU is busy, so these are physics-state contact sheets, not Unity video. The `.rep` files in D render
  with `eval/render_video.py` when the GPU is free.

Headline: A scores 0.613 [0.599, 0.627] (panel 0.615). Pre-SD games score 0.648 ± 0.019; SD games 0.580 ± 0.020.

## Failure modes, ranked by losses they explain

### F1. The sudden-death drowning lottery: 44% of all losses

- **Evidence (games.jsonl):**
  - 3030 of 5904 games (51%) reach sudden death.
  - 999 of A's 2250 losses (44%) are falls in sudden death. Another 345 are falls before it.
  - Within SD games, the opponent holding Gold mushroom drops A to 0.216 (residual −0.37 [−0.42, −0.32]). Platform drops it to
    0.290 (−0.29) and Beam to 0.395 (−0.19). When A holds them it scores 0.954, 0.819 and 0.732.
  - Of the 648 seed pairs lost on both sides, 308 are lost in SD on both sides.
  - The sim reports cause codes 2 and 0 for environment deaths. The replays show both are drownings (water, or the space
    floor at y −26).
- **Replays:** sudden death sinks the level's platforms below the water (e.g. platform 19 on map 18 ends at y −22). Whoever
  has no hover, platform or high perch drifts down and drowns.
  - [f5-seed311064](atlas/f5-seed311064-side0-map18-b.png): Magnet gun/Grenade/Black hole against Mine/Drill/Platform. Both
    camp 56u apart for 130 s, with bodies within 12u 1% of the time. In SD, A presses every button and drowns; B stands on its
    own Platform.
  - [a20-seed711446](atlas/a20-seed711446-side1-map18-b.png): the opponent has Gold mushroom. The bodies stay 74u apart for 137 s
    with close_frac 0.02. A sinks into the water while B floats at y 15.
  - [t5b-seed1111087](atlas/t5b-seed1111087-side0-map123-b.png): a space map. A spends 70 Portal gun activations from 57u
    away while B perches on a rock for 2 min, then A falls through the floor.
  - [t5b-seed1111356](atlas/t5b-seed1111356-side0-map105-b.png): 61u apart, and A drifts into the water with no platform
    under it.
  - [a20-seed711342](atlas/a20-seed711342-side1-map49-a.png) is a win of the same kind. A does not activate an ability for
    115 s at 75u, and B drowns first.
- **Sample (70 SD games of 120):**
  - The bodies' mean distance is 42u (median 39), and they are within 12u a median 5% of the time.
  - In 34 of the 70, the mean distance exceeds 40u.
  - A's longest idle stretch has a median of 56 s, and 100 s in the SD games A lost.
  - A sits a mean 5u above B in its wins and 6u below B in its losses.
- **Why it matters for humans:** humans never reach SD (0 of 393 human seats, OFFENSE §1.4). Half of the eval, and of the
  training signal, is a kit lottery on sinking platforms. A's edge is smallest there (0.580 vs 0.648 before SD).
- **Hypothesis:** the policy's dominant plan is to disengage and outlast. The endgame rewards owning a survival tool and the
  high ground, not fighting, so the training signal for engaging is diluted. This is consistent with OFFENSE §1.1-1.4, which
  is now confirmed by direct observation.
- **Cheap experiments:**
  1. Zero compute: re-score every E0 arm with SD games counted as draws (`failure_atlas.py report` already splits by phase).
     If arm rankings change, SD noise is masking offense effects, and the pre-SD score should be the primary strength metric.
     Here the panel drops from 0.615 to 0.573.
  2. CPU only: re-run the anneal vs final5 on 500 pairs at `--episode-ticks 6960`, with timeout = draw, and compare the draw rate
     and KH_PRE. This measures how often the bot can actually close out a game before SD.
  3. GPU, 1B steps: fine-tune from anneal with episodes capped at 6960 and timeout −1 (no SD). This tests whether removing the
     lottery raises pre-SD kill hazard.

### F2. Idle stretches and unused strong abilities: losses track A not pressing

- **Evidence (sample of 120 replays):**
  - In A's 39 losses, A's longest stretch without an activation is 52.5 s against B's 38.6 s (A−B +14.0 s [+1.9, +26.8]).
  - In A's 79 wins it is the reverse: −17.2 s [−28.4, −6.2].
  - Activations per minute: A 8.4 vs B 11.3 in losses, 13.2 vs 10.2 in wins.
  - In 14 of 120 games A never activated any ability. In 34 of 120, A idled for at least 60 s straight.
- **Replays:**
  - [f5-seed311426](atlas/f5-seed311426-side0-map21-b.png): A holds Throw/Beam/Mine, and both bodies hop 10u apart for 117 s.
    A makes one activation in 117 s, so Beam, a top-3 ability (+0.16 residual), is never fired. At SD the platforms go, and A
    falls.
  - [f5-seed311273](atlas/f5-seed311273-side1-map28-b.png): A holds Mine/Freeze ray/Teleport and presses nothing for 3.9 s
    while it closes to 0u. B's grenade kills it.
  - Contrast: [t5b-seed1111177](atlas/t5b-seed1111177-side0-map95-a.png), a clean win. A snipes with Beam from 36u above
    B, whose activations are zero.
- **Caveat:** this is descriptive and the direction is unclear. A losing position may cause idling as easily as idling causes
  losses.
- **Hypothesis:** the button heads are collapsed (OFFENSE §1.7, buttons entropy about 1% of max), so "don't press" is a
  stable fixed point in neutral positions.
- **Cheap experiment (no training):**
  - Run `all_combos.py` on the anneal vs final5 at seed base 311001 (the same games as here), with `--temperature-a 1,1.5,1.5`
    and with `1,0.7,0.7`. That is 1076 games each on 4 CPU workers.
  - Measure the score, plus idle and activations per minute via `failure_atlas.py replay`/`render --no-png` on 60 seeds.
  - If a flatter buttons/hold head raises activations without hurting the score, the collapse is costing offense. That would
    support running the I4 entropy floor as a main arm.

### F3. Opening deaths to one projectile, after wasted or no abilities

- **Evidence:**
  - 1072 games (18%) end in under 15 s, which is the regime humans play in (median human round 10.8 s). A loses 32% of them.
  - 151 of those 344 opening losses are credited opponent kills, and 114 are falls.
  - A's rate of dying this way is 0.46× the opponent's [0.36, 0.55], so A is relatively strongest here against bots.
- **Replays:**
  - [f5-seed311110](atlas/f5-seed311110-side1-map42-b.png): A holds Shrink ray/Tack/Shrink ray and fires all three slots in
    the first 2 s at 70u range. That is out of reach, and the cooldowns were burned. One Bow arrow kills it at 7.8 s.
  - [t5b-seed1111438](atlas/t5b-seed1111438-side0-map43-b.png): A holds Tesla coil/Black hole/Grenade and throws three
    grenades that land nowhere near B. It never uses Tesla or Black hole, stands under B, and dies to B's Spike at 12.4 s.
  - [a20-seed711199](atlas/a20-seed711199-side1-map11-b.png): A holds Gold mushroom, the best ability, yet descends onto B
    over open water, misses the only platform and drowns at 22 s.
  - [a20-seed711503](atlas/a20-seed711503-side1-map82-b.png): 20 s of hugging at 7.5u (close_frac 0.89) with nobody landing a
    kill, until B's Push squishes A.
  - Win, for contrast: [f5-seed311036](atlas/f5-seed311036-side0-map54-a.png). Both hug at 5u without pressing for 3 s, then
    A's Meteor kills.
- **Sample:** A's first activation comes at a median 5.1 s and 23u, against B's at 3.5 s and 30u. The opponents also fire
  early and from range, so this is not unique to A.
- **Hypothesis:** close-range ability use is poorly aimed and poorly timed: abilities are spent out of range, and the bot
  stands in the threat line. This fits OFFENSE §1.8 (the network barely reads projectiles).
- **Cheap experiment:**
  - Replay the 151 opening opponent-kill losses (`replay` takes `--pick seed:side` lists; about 3 min on 4 workers).
  - Classify each with `eval/loss_causes.py`-style fields: distance at death, A's presses in the last 2 s, and the killer
    projectile's age.
  - If more than half show A's slots on cooldown or never pressed while the threat flew for at least 0.5 s, then a dodge and
    timing curriculum (rusher-style opponents) is the lever, not reward.

### F4. Kit power, not bot skill, is the pre-game pocket structure

- **Confirmed pockets** (discovery residual, then confirmation residual, all with Holm < 0.01):
  - The opponent holds Gold mushroom: −0.249, confirmed −0.243 (excess loss 120 points over 482 games).
  - The opponent holds Platform: −0.163, confirmed −0.193.
  - The opponent holds Beam: −0.145, confirmed −0.162.
  - A holds Throw (−0.094), Tesla coil (−0.092), Web swing, Mine, Engine, Black hole or Portal gun (each −0.07 to −0.10, all
    confirmed).
- **These are kit power:**
  - Across the 36 abilities, the residual when A holds one and the residual when the opponent holds it correlate at r = −0.92.
  - No ability has a significant asymmetry (own + opp, every q ≥ 0.9). So A does not play any ability worse than the panel
    nets do.
- **Maps:** no map pocket survives confirmation. Level15 (map 14, held out) is −0.25 in discovery but −0.07 [−0.18, +0.04] out of
  sample.
- **Setups:** the within-pair score correlation is 0.24-0.40 by opponent, and pairs lost on both sides run 1.3-2.2× the rate
  expected under independence. A sizeable share of outcome variance is the setup (kits and map), and most of that is kit
  power.
- **Implication:** per-ability or per-map curricula are not supported by bot-vs-bot data. Against humans, kit choice (Gold
  mushroom, Platform, Beam) may matter more than policy changes in a single match.
- **Cheap experiment:** none for the bot. For humans, tabulate the human-game kits (S/aggression-human) against these
  residuals, to see whether human wins concentrate in human-held strong kits.

## What this does not show

- **Everything here is bot vs bot.** Humans play a fast, close game that this data barely samples. F3 is the closest proxy,
  and the human replays (eval/aggression_replays.py) remain the ground truth for "weak on offense against humans".
- **Behavioural statistics come from 120 replays,** which is wide enough for direction but not for fine effect sizes. The
  replay/render tools scale to any pick list.
- **Activation counts are the telemetry slot counters** (named dash/bow/platform for legacy reasons). Passive or held
  abilities may count differently from instant ones.
