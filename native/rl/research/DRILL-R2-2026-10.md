## 0. Summary

- **Round 1's style paid the bot to stay out of the ground.** Over the last 10% of training the four
  styled runs netted -21 to -39 (`style.terms.*.paid` summed, 1.47M learner decisions): `burrow_approach`
  +43 to +68, `drill_retreat` -81 to -124, `burrow_kill` +11 to +19 (Section 1.1). `drill_retreat` charges
  every air-phase retreat of a Drill form that has touched the ground (styles/drill_burrow.toml:27-28), and a
  dive-bomb that clips a platform then overshoots is exactly that. Replayed on SO-s7121's 1200 P1 games, the
  dominant episode type (a clip under 0.5 s, 1691 of 4063) nets -0.013; an episode that never touches the
  ground nets 0. Multiplying this style by 3-5 would multiply the tax.
- **Burrowing is not strategically dominated** (Section 1.3): underground is the safest place in a drill
  (0.5 opponent kills per 1000 decisions vs 1.1 in the air phase), long runs end in a kill as often as
  clips, and the reward never paid for them.
- **Matthew's launch claim holds where it matters, and the data says why it is rare** (Section 1.4). The
  ground adds speed (median gain entry -> last underground step +6.5 u/s for clips, +14 u/s for runs over
  1.5 s), aim is the strongest predictor of a kill at every distance (far exits within 30 deg of the opponent
  at >= 30 u/s kill 31% of the time vs 9% off-aim; next to the opponent 51% vs 15%), and the rarest exit in
  the data, a fast aimed exit after >= 0.5 s underground, is the most lethal one (56-61% of them kill, n = 50
  / 18 / 23, vs 45-48% for the same exit after a clip). One drill kill in six already comes from a surfacing
  beyond 16 u. What contradicts him: today's long burrows are the slowest exits (24-27 u/s vs 50 for a clip)
  because they are slow, curved entries; no flight in the data crosses a map (path p90 28 u, every kill within
  0.5 s of surfacing); and the launch, at 31%, is a worse bet than popping out beside the opponent (51%). The
  draft style paid a launch nothing (its surfacing term needed the opponent within 16 u); the revised one pays
  aim and speed, and the flight.
- **Senses: keep v4.3 this round, and build one sense for round 3 if D3 says the senses bind** (Sections
  1.2, 2.2). The exit distance along the heading is in the row; the bearing to the opponent relative to the
  heading and the exit velocity are functions of fields the row carries; what no column carries is the ground
  beyond the first surface, so the recommended sense is the line to the opponent (ground fraction and clear
  run-in along the segment body -> opponent), in the duplicate down-ray slot 35, under writer revision bit 64.
- **Recommended: `styles/drill_burrow2.toml`** (Section 2.1). No air charge. Net underground approach
  (telescoping) in the first run of a form; the form's first surfacing pays the run's length (0.075/s, capped
  at 2 s) times a launch quality = aimed (within 30 deg of the opponent) x max(near, fast); the flight after it
  pays each unit of new minimum distance at the approach rate, scaled by the burrow behind it; a 0.2
  burrow-kill premium for the form's underground time in the last 6 s; the unforced-death penalty. Bounded at
  ~0.5 per form plus the kill; 40 scripted scenarios pass in the C VM and the Python interpreter bit for bit,
  including the launch farm, the aim-and-never-arrive farm and the clip-then-dive case. At init (replayed on
  round 1's policies) it pays +43 per 1200 games on SO-s7121, 18% of the kill reward, every term positive, on
  42% of drill episodes.
- **Plan** (Section 2.3): five runs of 2.5B (2B constant + 0.5B anneal) from round 1's annealed endpoints,
  ~4-5 GPU-h: K (v4.3, no style), R2 x2 seeds, R2x2 (every positive term doubled), R2v42 (v4.2). P1 primary
  and unchanged; P2-P4 unchanged; a launch criterion L joins intent for the showcase (Section 2.4).

### 1.2 The senses

What round 1 established (spec 3, 4.1): v4.2 is blind underground (all rays 0, body block frozen); v4.3 gives
the form body's velocity and heading, and rays from inside report the exit distance (83-87% of underground
rows read the ground left along the heading within 1 u at the observation delay); the policy is recurrent
(minGRU, train/train.py:1, 1025), so a per-form timer is representable without a column.

**Probes on round 1's own P1 rows** (`drill-spec/probe.py` on `R1/SO-s7121/rows.npz` = v4.3 rows written at
bits 48, 9372 truly-underground rows in 1947 episodes, and on `R1/C/rows.npz` = v4.2 rows, 7554 rows in
1822 episodes; GroupKFold(5) by game, held-out R^2; `R2/probe/probe-*.txt`):

| Target | v4.3 (SO-s7121 rows) ridge / MLP | v4.2 (C rows) ridge / MLP |
|---|---|---|
| ground left along the heading (clip 20 u) | 0.17 / 0.18 | 0.12 / 0.05 |
| decisions to surfacing (clip 30) | 0.20 / 0.19 | 0.28 / 0.25 |
| heading sin | 0.89 / 0.75 | 0.72 / 0.59 |
| opponent dx, dy | 0.96 / 0.94, 0.96 / 0.93 | 0.95 / 0.91, 0.92 / 0.90 |

Reading: the same result as spec 4.1's probe. A ridge or one-layer MLP on the flat block cannot pick the ray
nearest the heading and un-delay it, so these numbers do not measure what the net can read; the direct
readout (`drill-spec/ray_heading.py`) does, and it is in the row. The probe says only that v4.3 did not make
the exit distance *linearly* legible, which a policy with a set encoder and recurrence does not need.
"Decisions to surfacing" is no better on v4.3 because the policies are different: SO's long runs are the
slow, curved ones, which are harder to time from one frame.

**What the behaviour says.** Under the same reward, SO burrowed about twice S on every count: episodes with
>= 1 s underground 1.7%/1.5% vs 0.8%/0.7%, kills after >= 0.5 s underground 10.3%/8.6% vs 5.5%/4.9%, P3 vs
me31 0.73/0.74 vs 0.66/0.65, P1 +0.023 (eval-only +-0.024). That is the only evidence that the senses bind,
and it was collected under a reward that paid against burrowing. It cannot separate "v4.3 lets it burrow" from
"v4.3 lets it dive better". Neither can any probe; a run can.

### 1.3 Is burrowing worth it at all?

From SO-s7121's P1 games (`R2/ep_stats.json`; C and S-s7111 in the same file agree):

- **Safety.** A deaths per 1000 decisions: 1.71 underground (16 in 9372: 10 ring-outs, 1 squish, 5 by the
  opponent = 0.53), 1.97 in the air phase of a drill (100 in 50,674: 56 by the opponent = 1.11, 30 ring-outs,
  13 self), 0.53 out of drill. Underground is the safest place in a drill from the opponent; the underground
  deaths that remain are ring-outs (riding a sinking platform, or drilling out of bounds), which
  `drill_unforced` already charges.
- **Speed.** The median speed at the k-th underground decision of a run: 29 u/s at k = 1 (1794 runs), 26 at
  k = 4, 20 at k = 6, 12.7 at k = 8 (220 runs), 15 at k = 15, 17 at k = 20, 21 at k >= 30 (48). This is
  selection, not deceleration: a dive enters at 30 u/s (45 x 0.66, spec 1.1) and is out of a 12 u platform
  in 0.4 s; the runs that last are the slow entries, which then gain 6 u/s^2. Air in a drill: median 35 u/s,
  p90 57. So a long burrow is 2-3x slower than the dive it replaces, and the policy has never had a reason
  to trade that time.
- **Payoff at the exit.** Surfacings grouped by the run just ended (n, within 10 u of the opponent, drill
  kill within 1 s, A death within 1 s, net closing during the run, exit speed): 1-3 decisions: 1056, 32%,
  29%, 2.2%, 3.6 u, 43 u/s; 4-7: 457, 42%, 28%, 3.1%, 6.8 u, 39; 8-14: 184, 27%, 26%, 4.9%, 7.3 u, 28;
  >= 15: 64, 34%, 25%, 6.3%, 10.6 u, 22. Long runs close more ground, exit slower, and are about as lethal at
  the exit.
- **Episode outcome** (table in 1.1): kill 36% / die 5.7% for >= 1 s runs vs 28% / 2.5% for clips vs 13% /
  3.2% for air-only dives. Confounded by where the policy chooses to dive, but at face value the reward
  already favours burrowing episodes (+8 kill points x 0.288 against +3 death points x 0.288) and a reward
  the size of half a kill per long run is enough to break any tie.

Verdict: long burrows are not dominated by dive-bombs; they are slower, safer, and as lethal. They were not
learned because nothing paid for them and one term charged their aftermath. If round 2's dose arms show no
response in burrowing (Section 2.4, D4), then burrowing is dominated for this opponent pool and the honest
statement is that a "ridiculously good" drill bot dive-bombs; a showcase that burrows anyway would then need
a style larger than the kill reward and would pay for it on P1.

### 1.4 Matthew's claim: "the best drill is willing to fly across the map because burrowing generates so much velocity"

Tested on round 1's P1 games (`drill-r2/launch_stats.py` -> `R2/launch_stats.json`; `aim_bins.py` ->
`R2/aim_bins.txt`). Per drill form, every underground run and the surfacing that ends it: the entry speed
(the step before the run), the speed of the last underground step and of the first air step (the player's
position over one decision = 4 ticks), the exit aim (cos of the angle between the exit step and the bearing
to the opponent; the last underground step's aim, which is what a style can see at the surfacing decision,
agrees with the first air step's at r = 0.98, median |diff| 0.015), the distance at the exit, and the flight
after it (air decisions until re-entry or the form's end). Underground = undelayed SDF < 0. SO-s7121 vs
main136 (1761 surfacings, 831 drill kills); C vs main136 (1378, 784) and SO-s7121 vs me31 (1389, 783) agree
unless quoted.

**1. Burrowing adds speed, but today's long burrows exit slow.** Median speeds (u/s) by the run's length,
entry -> last underground step -> first air step, and the median gain over the run:

| Run | n | entry | last underground | first air | gain | kill in the form | A dies in the flight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-3 decisions (<= 0.2 s) | 1056 | 35.6 | 43.2 | 49.7 | +6.5 | 30% | 2.0% |
| 4-7 | 457 | 28.3 | 38.8 | 43.4 | +9.1 | 30% | 3.1% |
| 8-14 (0.5-0.9 s) | 184 | 18.9 | 28.0 | 28.4 | +10.0 | 31% | 4.3% |

The +6 u/s^2 (spec 1.1) is real and the gain grows with the run; but the runs that last are the slow entries
(an entry at 45 u/s is through a 12 u platform in 0.4 s), so the long run's exit, 24-27 u/s, is half a clip's.
The exit the claim describes, a fast entry held underground for 2 s, would leave at (30 + 12) x 1.5 = 63
u/s, above a clip's p90 (67 at the first air step): the mechanic supports him, the behaviour has not
produced it. Runs of >= 23 decisions: 16 in 1761 surfacings.

**2. Aim and speed predict a kill at range; the burrow-built launch is the most lethal exit in the data.**
Kill in the same form after a surfacing, by exit aim (last underground step vs the bearing to the opponent)
and distance (SO-s7121; C in `R2/aim_bins.txt`):

**3. Kills come right after the surfacing; the 3 s kill window cut nothing.** Of 831 drill kills, 413 had no
surfacing in the form before them (killed from underground or on the first exit step) and 660 were airborne
at the kill. For the 418 with a surfacing: time from the last surfacing to the kill p10/50/90 = 0 / 0.13 /
0.40 s, > 1 s 0.5%, > 3 s 0; distance to the opponent at that surfacing p10/25/50/75/90 = 5.0 / 6.6 / 9.6 /
13.7 / 20.6 u, beyond 16 u 16.7% (C 15.1%, me31 11.8%), beyond 25 u 5.5% (2.8%, 1.6%). The draft's
`count_over(180, ug)` lost 0 of the 54 kills that followed a run of >= 8 decisions, as would a 6 s window;
the revision moves to 6 s bounded by the form anyway, because the reward is meant to create flights that do
not exist yet.

**4. How often round 1 launches now.** Of SO-s7121's 1387 first surfacings, 19.8% [17.8, 22.0] leave at >=
30 u/s within 30 deg of an opponent beyond 16 u (C 12.8%, me31 11.5%); 26% are aimed beyond 16 u at any
speed; 3.6% are launches after a run of >= 8 decisions (C 1.5%, me31 2.0%).

**Verdict.** Supported in the part that matters: speed and aim are what make an exit lethal at range, and the
burrow-built launch is the single most lethal exit the bot has, which it produces 1.5-3.6% of the time
because nothing paid for it and because its long runs are slow entries. Contradicted in the picture: no
flight crosses a map, every kill lands within half a second of surfacing, and a far launch (31%) is a worse
bet than a pop-out beside the opponent (51%). Consequences for the design: the surfacing pay keys on aim and
speed with no distance requirement (the draft's 16 u gate paid a launch nothing); the flight after a burrow
is paid for closing, so a launch that arrives is worth more than one that does not; the kill premium credits
the whole form (6 s); and a launch criterion L joins the showcase (2.4).

## 2. Recommended design

### 2.1 Style `drill_burrow2` (`styles/drill_burrow2.toml`)

Design rules, from Section 1:

1. Never charge an air phase. The only negative term is the unforced-death penalty (plus losses inside an
   underground run, which the telescoping approach term charges in full).
2. Pay duration only when it ends usefully: at the form's first surfacing if it is aimed (and either fast or
   next to the opponent), in the flight after it for ground actually closed, or at a drill kill. Everything
   is capped and once per form (or once per unit of new minimum distance), so circling is bounded by the 4 s
   cooldown.
3. Zero in sudden death and with no living opponent. On for the whole run (no fade), as round 1.

```toml
name = "drill_burrow2"
kits = [[26, 2, 2], [2, 26, 2], [2, 2, 26]]

[let]
ug = "any(entities where owner == self and underground == 1)"
drilling = "form_ability(self) == ability.Drill"
live = "header.sudden_death == 0 and alive(opp)"
closer = "prev(min(dist(self, opp), 30)) - min(dist(self, opp), 30)"   # approach since the last decision, u
surfaced = "drilling and not ug and prev(ug)"                            # the decision an underground run ends on
run_ticks = "prev(since(not ug))"                                        # at a surfacing: the run that just ended, ticks
no_surfacing_yet = "since(surfaced) >= since_form_entry(self)"           # this form has not surfaced (first run; >= so never == never holds)
first_surfacing = "prev(since(surfaced)) >= since_form_entry(self)"      # this surfacing is the form's first
surfaced_in_form = "since(surfaced) < since_form_entry(self)"            # a surfacing has happened in this form
# exit velocity (u/s) from the player's position over the last decision: at a surfacing, the step that leaves the ground
vx = "(self.x - prev(self.x)) * 60 / max(dt, 1)"
vy = "(self.y - prev(self.y)) * 60 / max(dt, 1)"
speed = "hypot(vx, vy)"
aim = "(vx * (opp.x - self.x) + vy * (opp.y - self.y)) / (max(speed, 1) * max(dist(self, opp), 1))"   # cos(exit angle to the opponent)
aimed = "clamp((aim - 0.5) / 0.366, 0, 1)"                               # 0 at 60 deg off the opponent, 1 within 30 deg
fast = "clamp((speed - 15) / 20, 0, 1)"                                  # 0 at 15 u/s, 1 at 35 u/s (before the x1.5 exit boost)
near = "clamp((16 - dist(self, opp)) / 10, 0, 1)"                        # 1 within 6 u of the opponent, 0 beyond 16 u
launch = "aimed * max(near, fast)"                                       # a launch is aimed, and either fast or already there
runmin = "-max_over(600, -min(dist(self, opp), 30))"                     # the closest the opponent has been in the last 10 s, u
progress = "prev(runmin) - runmin"                                       # > 0 only on a decision that sets a new minimum
ug_form = "min(count_over(360, ug), since_form_entry(self) / 4)"         # underground decisions in the last 6 s, none from an earlier form
surf_run = "surfaced * run_ticks"                                        # the run just ended at a surfacing, else 0
held = "min(max_over(600, surf_run) / 60, 2) / 2"                        # the longest run a surfacing ended in the last 10 s: 0.1 at 0.2 s, 1 at 2 s

[[term]]   # dense: net underground approach in the first run of a form; gains <= 2 u a decision, losses in full
name = "burrow_approach"
when = "live and ug and prev(ug) and no_surfacing_yet"
value = "0.006 * min(closer, 2)"   # <= 0.18 for a 30 u approach

[[term]]   # escrow: the first surfacing of a form pays the run's length (0.075 per s, full at 2 s) times the launch quality
name = "burrow_surface"
when = "live and surfaced and first_surfacing"
value = "0.075 * min(run_ticks / 60, 2) * launch"   # <= 0.15 per form

[[term]]   # the flight after a surfacing: every u of new minimum distance to the opponent, in the air, at the approach rate
           # times the burrow behind it (a 0.2 s clip's dive earns a tenth of the rate, a 2 s burrow's launch the full rate)
name = "burrow_flight"
when = "live and drilling and not ug and not surfaced and surfaced_in_form"
value = "0.006 * clamp(progress, 0, 2) * held"   # <= 0.18 per 10 s; a loop in the air pays nothing and costs nothing

[[term]]   # burrow-kill premium: drill kill with >= 0.5 s underground in this form in the last 6 s, full (0.2) at 2 s
name = "burrow_kill"
on = "death"
when = "ev.actor == self and ev.subject != self and ev.source_ability == ability.Drill and header.sudden_death == 0 and ug_form >= 8"
value = "0.2 * min(1, ug_form / 30)"

[[term]]   # unforced drill death (ring-out, self) costs extra
name = "drill_unforced"
on = "death"
when = "ev.subject == self and form_ability(self) == ability.Drill and (ev.cause == death.RING_OUT or ev.cause == death.SELF)"
value = "-0.08"
```

Where the ramps come from (Section 1.4): `aimed` is 0 at 60 deg and 1 at 30 deg because the kill rate steps
up between the 45-60 and 30-45 deg bins at every distance (9% -> 22% at 6-16 u, 7% -> 17% beyond) and
again at 15-30 deg; `fast` ramps 15 -> 35 u/s on the last underground step (before the x1.5 exit boost)
because far aimed exits below 20 u/s kill 16%, 20-30 u/s 26%, >= 30 u/s 31%, and reach 6 u at 12% / 7% /
19%; `near` keeps the pop-out beside the opponent (the 51% exit) paid at any speed, but only when aimed (the
off-aim pop-out kills 15%, and pays 0). The flight term's `held` factor is what stops the term from paying
the dive-bomb the bot already does: a clip of 3 decisions scales it to 0.1.

How the history slots make this work (the DSL has no per-form accumulator; `train/style_compile.py:852-889`
compiles `prev/since/count_over/max_over` into ring-buffer slots, and `train/bopl_style.h:741-757` updates
every slot *before* the terms run, in compile order, so a slot that reads another is updated after it; the
Python interpreter checks that order, `eval/style_score.py:88`):

- `run_ticks = prev(since(not ug))`: at the surfacing decision `since(not ug)` is already 0, and `prev` of it
  is the value one decision earlier, the ticks since the last above-ground decision, i.e. the run's length.
  A 24-decision run reads 96 ticks (test `approach_surface`).
- `first_surfacing = prev(since(surfaced)) >= since_form_entry(self)`: the previous surfacing was before this
  form began (or never: `BS_NEVER` is 1e9, `train/bopl_style.h:78,598`). A hop chain's second surfacing fails
  it (test `second_surfacing`); a new form passes it again (`new_form`).
- `no_surfacing_yet` gates the approach term to the form's first run, which is what blocks the hop-chain dip
  farm without charging the air (test `hop_chain_dip`: 20 dip cycles pay 0.042 in total, all from the first
  cycle).
- `vx, vy` are finite differences of the player's position (`self.x` follows the form; `body(self).vx` is the
  slime record, frozen at form entry, so it cannot be used); `dt` is the ticks since the previous decision (4).
- `runmin` is the minimum distance over a 10 s window (`max_over` of the negated, clipped distance), and
  `progress` is positive only on a decision that lowers it: a loop in the air, a hop chain's second run and a
  dive that merely repeats a distance already reached pay 0 (tests `flight_oscillation`, `launch_farm`,
  `hop_chain_dip`). The window includes the underground approach, so the flight pays only below the depth
  the approach term already paid for; approach + flight together are bounded by 0.006 x 30 u per 10 s.
- `ug_form = min(count_over(360, ug), since_form_entry(self) / 4)`: underground decisions in the last 6 s,
  capped at the decisions of this form, so a kill 4 s after a previous form's long burrow is not credited
  for it (test `prev_form_kill`) and a 3.4 s flight before the kill is (test `long_flight_kill`, which the
  draft's 3 s window paid 0).
- `held = max_over(600, surfaced * run_ticks)`: the run length is only nonzero at the surfacing decision, so
  the window's maximum carries it through the flight (and through a flicker, taking the longer part).

Compiled: 2400 bytes, 13 slots, 821 history floats per player, max stack 7, 484 code words (limits: 64 slots,
4096 floats, `style_compile.py:103`); the two 10 s windows cost two 151-entry scans per decision per player.
`make style-check`'s agreement run passes on 40 replay dumps (205,740 decisions) for both files
(`R2/style_check.txt`).

### 2.2 Observation: v4.3 this round; the sense to build for round 3

**The one sense to build, if D3 says the senses are the bottleneck: the line to the opponent.** Layout v4.4,
writer revision bit `BOPL4_ABLATE_LINE = 64` (`train/bopl_rows_v4.h:46` enum; `--v4-ablate 112` = 16 | 32 |
64; `league/registry.py:385` `WRITER_REVISIONS = 16 | 32 | 64` so weights load across and sidecars record it;
`train/bank_kits.py` WriterBackend keeps each frozen member's own bits as now).

- *Where in the row:* ray slot 35 (`BOPL4_RAYS_AROUND + 3`, the -45 deg down ray, `train/bopl_layout_v4.h:47`),
  floats 484-491 of the row (`BOPL4_OBS_RAY_OFFSET` 204 + 35 x 8). At bits 48 that slot duplicates around ray
  28 (`bopl_layout_v4.h:73-76`: the drill lineage never ran with bit 8, v4.1's aim/LOS rays, which reuse the
  same slot), so nothing is lost and the row's width and every other field keep their meaning, as v4.3 did.
  Bit 64 with bit 8 is an error (assert at `bopl4_ablation()`); bit 64 with the fan ablated (bit 2 set) writes zeros.
- *What is computed* (in `bopl_rays_v4.h`'s `bopl4f_write`, next to `bopl4f_aim_rays`, from the same
  `Bopl4Scene`): the segment from the body (`s->x, s->y`: the form body in v4.3, else the slime) to the primary
  enemy's centre (`s->enemy_x, s->enemy_y`, the delayed state's, exactly `bopl4f_los`'s source); zero the
  whole slot unless `s->flags & BOPL4F_ENEMY` and the length `L = bopl4f_sqrt(ex*ex + ey*ey) > 1e-9f`; direction
  `dx = bopl4f_div(ex, L), dy = bopl4f_div(ey, L)`.
  - `[0]` the march toward the enemy: `bopl4f_march` with the fan's arguments (the group mask and limit the
    fan ray would use at that direction, `exit = (s->flags & BOPL4F_INSIDE) != 0`, max `BOPL4_RAY_LENGTH`),
    then `min(t, L)` over `BOPL4_RAY_LENGTH`: from outside, the air before the first wall on the way (1 when
    the opponent is nearer than any wall within 40 u); from inside, the exit distance toward the opponent.
    This is v4.1's LOS distance feature restricted to walls; reuse that cast.
  - `[1]` ground fraction: 32 samples at `t_k = L * (((float)k + 0.5f) * 0.03125f)`, k = 0..31, point
    `(s->x + dx * t_k, s->y + dy * t_k)`, ground when `bopl4f_sdf(s, bopl4f_all(s), x, y) < 0.0f` (walls only,
    the full mask, no reach culling; no circles, water or blast planes); `[1] = (float)count * 0.03125f`.
  - `[2]` clear run-in: the number of samples after the last ground sample, times 0.03125 (1 when the line is
    clear, 0 when the opponent's end is in ground).
  - `[3..7]` zero.
  Units: fractions in [0, 1], `[0]` over 40 u like every ray; nothing needs clipping beyond `min(t, L)`.
- *What CPU and CUDA must agree on* (the gate is `make encode-check` at bits 0/16/32/48/112 with a negative
  control, as round 1 ran it, spec 4.1; host `-ffp-contract=off`, kernel `-fmad=false`, Makefile:279): the
  enemy source and the `BOPL4F_ENEMY` guard; `bopl4f_sqrt`/`bopl4f_div` (not `sqrtf`, `rsqrtf` or `/`); the
  evaluation order `L * ((k + 0.5) * 0.03125)` then `x + dx * t` as two separate operations; the same
  `bopl4f_sdf` with the same mask (`bopl4f_all`) and its wall order (`w < d ? w : d` over the aligned then the
  rotated walls); the strict `< 0.0f`; integer counts converted once; the march's arguments identical to the
  fan ray's at that slot (the CUDA kernel `bopl_rays_v4.cu:1013,1114` already passes the inside flag). Cost:
  32 SDF evaluations, about one extra ray in 36.
- *Why this one.* The other two candidates are functions of fields v4.3 already carries: the opponent's
  bearing relative to the heading is a rotation of the heading (sin/cos in the self body block, probe R^2
  0.89 linearly) and the opponent offset (0.96); the predicted exit point along the heading is the fan ray
  nearest the heading (within 5 deg; from inside it marches to the exit, 83-87% within 1 u), and the exit
  speed is `(body speed + 6 x time to exit) x 1.5`, two row fields through a fixed nonlinearity. What the row
  cannot express is anything past the first surface: from outside every ray ends at the first wall, from
  inside at the first exit, and the terrain block's twelve scalars are all local. The static-collider entity
  rows describe each wall on its own (centre, extents, rotation, relative to the observer), and the set
  encoder is a per-row MLP with max/mean pooling (`bopl_layout_v4.h:13`): "does the segment to the opponent
  cross this rectangle" needs the opponent's position inside the wall's row, which it does not have, so the
  pooled sum cannot assemble the ground fraction either. The line to the opponent is what a launch decides
  on: whether the exit along the bearing has clear air to the target, and whether a tunnel route exists.
- *Could the net already derive it from v4.3?* Partly, with recurrence: a policy that has watched the fan
  for a second remembers the walls it has seen, so the ground between itself and an opponent it has flown
  past is recoverable from history; the ground on a line it has never scanned is not, and the fan (10 deg
  apart, 40 u) is one frame's worth of walls, not a map. The probe cannot test this (1.2); the run can:
  if the v4.4 arm in round 3 raises L and P2 over v4.3 with the same reward, the sense binds.

## 3. Cheaper and alternative designs

**A. Minimal patch (1 run, ~1 GPU-h): round 1's style minus `drill_retreat`.** Keep `burrow_approach`
gated to the form's first run (the `no_surfacing_yet` gate above, which is what makes dropping the retreat
charge safe against the dip farm) and raise `burrow_kill` to 0.2. No surfacing or flight term. Trade-off:
the only duration signal left is the kill premium at 2e-4 fires per decision, so the >= 1 s half of P2 is
likely still missed; it is the cheapest test of "was the tax the whole problem". The replay at init gives it
+24 per 1200 games on SO-s7121 (approach + kill, Section 2.1 table), 10% of the kill reward.

**B. Matthew's "bigger bonus": round 1's style x3-5.** Not recommended: the net is negative (1.1), so x3-5
taxes ground contact three to five times harder; and it has no duration term to scale. If wanted, drop
`drill_retreat` first, at which point it is A at a larger scale. Round 1's own hack tests then fail on the dip
farm unless the first-run gate is added.

**C. Per-surfacing escrow.** `drill_burrow2` with `first_surfacing` dropped from `burrow_surface`: every
aimed surfacing pays its run's length. It credits hop chains directly (the showcase movement) at the cost of
a larger bound: 0.15 per hop, or 0.075 per underground second, with no cooldown between hops, which is 15
kills per minute at the rate. A rate limit ("no eligible surfacing in the last 4 s") is expressible with
`count_over(240, ...) <= 1` but keys on eligible rather than paid surfacings, so a regular hop chain is paid
once and then never. If Matthew prefers to see hop chains paid, run C as the fifth arm instead of R2x2 and
add the kills/min veto weight; the test file needs one scenario changed (`second_surfacing` expects a second
pay) and one added (a 60 s hop chain's bound).

**D. The sense now.** Section 2.2's definition, ~0.5-1 agent-day plus round 1's gate list (encode_check CPU
== CUDA at bits 0/16/32/48/112 with a negative control, env_bench hashes with the bit off, check_v4 --build,
check_style_binding, the drill-gate smoke run), then a sixth arm R2v44 (bits 112, drill_burrow2, SO-s7121
init; weights load across the revision). Not in round 2 unless the build lands before the queue reaches the
fifth run: D3 decides whether it is needed for the price of one run, and a sixth arm is ~1 GPU-h.
